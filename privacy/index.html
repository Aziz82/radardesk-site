RadarDesk

Privacy Policy

Effective 6 August 2026

RadarDesk is an iOS app that presents public regulatory filings and US market data for one purpose: answering what a company’s insiders and the short and off-exchange tape have been doing in a given stock. This page explains what the app does and does not do with data about you.

It is written to be read, not to be skimmed past. If something here is unclear, email aldubaib@gmail.com and it will be corrected.

The short version

No account, no analytics, no tracking, no ads.

RadarDesk never asks for your name, email address or any other personal detail. There is nothing to sign up for, no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, and no third-party code of any kind in the app.

Where the two tiers differ is in what leaves your phone. On the free tier the app fetches public filings straight from the regulators and does the scoring on the device; nothing about you reaches RadarDesk’s own service, because the app never contacts it. On Pro the app talks to RadarDesk’s server, which holds an anonymous device token, the ticker symbols you follow and your alert settings — and nothing else. Both are set out in full below.

What RadarDesk never collects, on either tier

  • No personal information. No name, email address, phone number, postal address or date of birth is requested or stored.
  • No account. There is no sign-up, no sign-in and no password. The app is fully usable the moment it is installed.
  • No advertising or tracking identifiers. No advertising identifier (IDFA), no IDFV, no fingerprinting. RadarDesk does not use the App Tracking Transparency permission because it has nothing to ask for.
  • No analytics. No usage measurement, session recording, funnel tracking or event logging. No analytics SDK is present in the app.
  • No crash reporting SDK. If you have Apple’s own “Share iPhone Analytics” setting enabled at the system level, iOS may send Apple a crash report; that is Apple’s mechanism, governed by Apple’s privacy policy, and the app has no access to it and cannot see it.
  • No third-party SDKs. The app has zero third-party dependencies. Nobody else’s code runs in it, so nobody else can collect anything through it.
  • No advertising. RadarDesk shows no ads and does not track you across apps or websites.
  • No location, contacts, photos, microphone, camera, health or motion data. The app never requests these permissions.
  • No payment information. If Pro is offered as a paid subscription it is sold through Apple’s in-app purchase system, which does not disclose your payment details to developers. RadarDesk never sees a card number.

The free tier: the work happens on your phone

On the free tier RadarDesk contacts no RadarDesk server. There is no device token, no watchlist sync and no push delivery, because there is nothing on the other end. The app downloads the same public files a person could download in a browser — SEC EDGAR filings, FINRA short volume, daily price bars — and runs the scoring on the device.

Two consequences are worth stating plainly:

  • Those publishers see your device’s IP address, as any website you visit does. RadarDesk does not control what they log. The SEC additionally requires every automated request to carry a contact address in its header; the app sends the developer’s contact address, never yours.
  • Off-exchange (dark pool) volume needs a credential the app cannot safely carry, so that panel is absent on the free tier rather than filled with zeros. The score is computed from the panels that are present and the app says so on screen.

Pro: what the server holds

Pro moves the fetching and scoring to RadarDesk’s own service, which is what makes push alerts and full-universe scanning possible. That requires the app to talk to it. Here is exactly what that involves.

One anonymous device token. On first launch the app asks the server for a random token and stores it in the iOS keychain. The server keeps only a one-way hash of it, so it cannot reconstruct the token from its own database. That token is the only thing that identifies your installation.

Held against that token:

  • The ticker symbols you follow, so the server can compute scores for them.
  • Your alert settings — which alert types, which thresholds, quiet hours.
  • Your time zone name (for example Asia/Riyadh), which is what makes quiet hours and the daily digest arrive at the right local time.
  • The platform and app version, so a bug can be traced to a build.
  • Your Apple push token, but only once you have granted notification permission. It is the address Apple routes a notification to; it is issued by Apple, changes on reinstall, and is useful for nothing else.

Nothing else. What the device token is not:

  • It is not linked to your identity. It is not derived from and not associated with your name, email address, phone number, Apple ID, IDFA, IDFV or any other identifier of you as a person. The server has no way to turn it into a person, because it never receives anything that would let it.
  • It is not used for tracking. It is never shared with, sold to, or combined with data from any advertiser, data broker or third party.
  • It is not a login. There is still no account. Reinstalling the app issues a new token, and the old watchlist is not recoverable — that is a deliberate consequence of not identifying you.

Ordinary server logs. Like any web service, the data service keeps short-lived operational logs — request path, timestamp, response status, and IP address as seen by the server — for security and debugging. These are retained no longer than 30 days and are not used to build a profile of you.

Deletion. Deleting the app orphans the token; the server-side record is deleted after 90 days of inactivity. To have it deleted sooner, email aldubaib@gmail.com with the token, which the app shows in Settings, and it will be removed.

What stays on your device

Your settings and your watchlist are saved locally, in the app’s own storage, using Apple’s standard UserDefaults mechanism. That is: whether you completed onboarding, whether you acknowledged the disclaimer, your watchlist of ticker symbols, your sort order, and your scanner filter preferences.

On the free tier this data never leaves your device at all. On Pro, the watchlist and alert settings are also held on the server as described below, so that alerts can be evaluated while the app is closed; the rest stays local either way. All of it is included in your device backup if you back up your iPhone (encrypted or not, according to your own backup settings). Deleting the app deletes the local copy. Settings ▸ Reset local data clears it without deleting the app.

Notifications

The app asks permission to send notifications. You can decline, and everything else continues to work — alerts still appear on the Alerts screen when you open the app; they simply are not pushed to you.

Granting permission causes iOS to issue an Apple push token, which the app sends to the server so that a notification has somewhere to go. Notification content is delivered through Apple’s Push Notification service; Apple therefore handles it in transit, under Apple’s own privacy policy. Notifications carry a ticker symbol and a score change, and nothing about you.

Withdrawing the permission in iOS Settings stops delivery immediately. The stored push token is cleared when Apple reports it as no longer valid, or on request.

Where the data in the app comes from

RadarDesk does not produce market data. It presents data published by US regulators and self-regulatory organisations, all of it public and free to access.

PublisherWhat is used
SEC EDGAR Form 4 insider transaction filings, and company facts including shares outstanding. EDGAR filings are public records published by a US government agency and are in the public domain.
FINRA Daily short volume files, and the OTC Transparency (ATS / off-exchange) weekly summaries. FINRA is a self-regulatory organisation; these datasets are published for public use.
Market data Daily price and volume bars from a market data provider, used to give the filings context.

RadarDesk is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by the SEC, FINRA, or any exchange or issuer. Regulator and organisation names appear here only to identify the origin of the data.

Data is presented with its publication cadence and lag stated on screen, always. Off-exchange volume in particular is published two to four weeks after the fact and is labelled as such everywhere it appears.

Your rights

On the free tier there is nothing to request access to, correct, export or delete: no record of your installation exists anywhere, because the app never contacts RadarDesk’s service.

On Pro, if you are covered by the GDPR, the UK GDPR, the CCPA/CPRA or a comparable law, you may ask what is held against your device token, ask for it to be deleted, and object to its processing. Email aldubaib@gmail.com. Requests are answered within 30 days. Note that because the token is not linked to your identity, the only way to identify your record is the token itself, shown in the app under Settings.

RadarDesk does not sell or share personal information, as those terms are used in the CCPA/CPRA, and never has.
